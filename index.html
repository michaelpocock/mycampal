Key features

myCampal for the Ford Tourneo Custom — 1200 × 715 × 590 mm, floor-mounted on two cleats.

Sleeping

Bed, 2000 mm
Panels run out over the drawers, with the far end resting either on the folded seat backs where the seats are still fitted, or on the fold-out legs and the top of the main table where they are not. Side wings fold down to 1550 mm across.
Lounger
The forward panel is split down the centreline, and each half lifts on its own ply prop into a backrest, so one side can sit up while the other stays flat. The overlay board stays flat beneath them.

Drawers

1Kitchen drawer
Twin-burner stove on the upper deck with a windshield, a fold-down side table front, and a side-opening tray at full extension. The tray is split into three by two loose lengthwise dividers that tip up and draw out.
2Storage drawer
Deep open crate with hand-hole cut-outs, 240 mm high. Two removable full-width dividers drop between cleats at any of three depths.
3Prep drawer
Two lift-off ply lids: chopping boards one way up, steel trivet bars on the reverse. Beneath them, two removable cross dividers drop between cleats, with room for three mugs in the middle compartment.
4Fridge drawer
Compressor fridge on four wheels, running out off the tailgate behind a latched front. The drawer's 150 mm sides keep the fridge easy to reach, and its back lifts out of two channels so the fridge can be rolled out of the cab end from inside the van.
5Middle-bay door
Lifts out on a hand hole at the top edge, then slides into runner channels at either of two heights — half in, half proud. Three steel bars across its face take a hot pan.

Tables

Fold-out leaf table
Pedestal table on two posts for the seating bay: a fixed centre panel with a leaf hinged along each long edge, folding up and over to meet on the centreline. It splits across the width into two halves, joined by toggle latches underneath, so half of it stands on one post when the second row of seats is out. It stows behind the cab, lifting clear of the seat backs on the way.
Swivel-arm bedside table
A square post drops into a clamp bracket on the right-hand divider at the cab end of the hatch bay, and a horizontal arm on the post head swings the top left, aft or right over the bed. The hatch panel is bored for the post, so it can stay fitted or come out. Stowed, the top and the leg clip flat to the cab-end face.

Elsewhere in the box

Through-bay webbing
Elastic cord crossing between the bay dividers on D-rings and karabiners, so a load is held in at the cab end and unclips in one pull. The bay stays open front and back for gear passed through from the cab.
Travel mode
Every panel folds flat with the wings nesting and the legs stowing into the stack, level with the deck. The three mattress pads stack on top, held by a buckled webbing strap across their width.
